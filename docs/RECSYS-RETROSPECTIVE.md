# What the first exam preparation taught us

This retrospective records process lessons from a beginner recommender-systems preparation session. It is a tutor synthesis, not a transcript or a reconstruction of the exam.

## What failed

The learner requested preparation across the course under a tight time budget and relied on explanations in chat. The tutor introduced broad topics but leaned too heavily on the supplied practice paper to decide depth and readiness. “Final review” was announced without a full materials-to-teaching coverage audit. Mentioning a topic, generating notes, and answering a familiar multiple-choice question were sometimes treated as stronger evidence than warranted.

The learner later reported an exam question about what was probably SVD versus SGD. Exact wording was not remembered. This does not establish a wrong answer or prove the question was absent from all teaching; it illustrates why conceptual contrasts must be checked explicitly. The learner expected a good result, but no verified grade is recorded here.

## Rules carried forward

1. Full source inventory precedes the teaching route. Every in-scope topic needs a place in the map even if the practice paper never mentions it.
2. A time limit changes priorities transparently. It does not authorize silently narrowing the requested course coverage.
3. A progress question is not permission to accelerate. Maintain explanations and examples unless the learner requests a change.
4. Teach prerequisites visibly before assessing them. A sentence mentioning a parameter is insufficient preparation for a question about its role.
5. Separate generated content, delivered teaching, coached performance, independent application, transfer and delayed recall.
6. Preserve the exact answer before interpreting it. Ambiguous wording, token labels and marking slips must not become invented conceptual errors.
7. Withdraw flawed questions and affected grades. Do not retrofit rubrics after seeing the answer.
8. Compare complete objectives and scores: fit versus penalty and reward estimate versus uncertainty bonus. A correct number with an unsupported explanation needs precise feedback.
9. Distinguish historical evaluation from training and distinguish validation model selection from final test estimation. Return to an example when the distinction remains unclear.
10. Practice-paper rules are evidence about that document. Verify actual exam logistics independently before stating them as current requirements.
11. Reconcile coverage against all materials before announcing final review. Report gaps and limitations without predicting a grade.
12. Save a concrete next action across sessions. Keep original practice scores and reported corrections separate.

## What to retain

Short explanations, complete worked examples, small checks, a durable evidence trail, source references and correction of teaching mistakes were useful. The change is to strengthen planning and coverage checks around that interaction, not replace it with a larger lecture dump.
